\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

We briefly review multi-agent communication learning, LLM-based decision
systems, policy distillation, and multi-UAV planning, and position TAGA-LLM
relative to these lines of work.

\paragraph{Communication in MARL.}
CommNet~\cite{sukhbaatar2016learning} broadcasts mean-pooled messages;
TarMAC~\cite{das2019tarmac} adds attention-based targeting;
DGN~\cite{jiang2020graph} uses graph convolutions but assumes a static
adjacency matrix.  QMIX~\cite{rashid2018qmix} and
MAPPO~\cite{yu2022surprising} address credit assignment without modeling
physical communication constraints.  Our TAGA mechanism injects three
physics-informed factors directly into the attention computation, treating the
graph as a dynamic, quality-variable channel.

\paragraph{LLMs for decision-making.}
ReAct~\cite{yao2023react} interleaves reasoning with actions;
Reflexion~\cite{shinn2023reflexion} adds self-reflection;
Voyager~\cite{wang2023voyager} generates executable code;
SayCan~\cite{ahn2022saycan} grounds LLM plans in affordances.  These
approaches either operate in single-agent settings or rely on pre-defined
protocols, and none encodes topology-dependent multi-agent states into LLM
prompts while preserving MARL-learned cooperative strategies.

\paragraph{Knowledge distillation for policy transfer.}
Policy distillation compresses a teacher policy into a smaller
student~\cite{rusu2016policy}.  Recent efforts explore distilling RL-learned
behaviors into language models for
interpretability~\cite{brown2020language,li2023camel}, yet existing pipelines
do not account for graph-structured multi-agent communication or
topology-dependent state encoding.  Our Stage~II distillation combines
LoRA-based parameter efficient fine-tuning~\cite{hu2022lora} with
topology-aware prompt encoding and chain-of-thought supervision to preserve
cooperative strategies in the distilled LLM.

\paragraph{Multi-UAV planning.}
Classical methods include potential fields~\cite{ge2002dynamic}, auction-based
allocation~\cite{choi2009consensus}, and metaheuristics~\cite{dorigo2006ant}.
MARL-based approaches such as MADDPG~\cite{lowe2017multi} and
HAPPO~\cite{kuba2022trust} train end-to-end policies, but for homogeneous
swarms, parameter-sharing MAPPO is more efficient~\cite{yu2022surprising}.  Our
work builds on MAPPO with TAGA for communication-aware coordination and bridges
the MARL--LLM gap through policy distillation.
