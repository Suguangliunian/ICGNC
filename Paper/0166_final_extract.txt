Decoupled Geometric Control of the Quadrotor
UA V Based on INDI
Jinghe Hu and Bin Xian ∗
School of Electrical and Information Engineering, Tianjin University, Tianjin 300072,
P. R. China,
2021234192@tju.edu.cn, xbin@tju.edu.cn
Abstract. This paper introduces a novel decoupled geometric control
method for quadrotor UAVs, based on the principles of incremental non-
linear dynamic inversion (INDI). The proposed method leverages differ-
ential flatness and INDI to linearize the nonlinearity of the UAV sys-
tem, allowing for effective control of both translational and rotational
movements. By applying INDI, the controller achieves strong tracking
capabilities, particularly for large-angle yaw movements, while ensuring
asymptotic convergence of attitude and position tracking errors. To val-
idate the effectiveness of the proposed controller, simulations comparing
it with an undecoupled geometric controller are conducted. The results
demonstrate that the decoupled geometric control algorithm offers con-
sistent performance and precise trajectory tracking.
Keywords: Decoupled Geometric Control, Incremental Nonlinear Dy-
namic Inversion, Nonlinear Control
1 Introduction
Quadrotor unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have recently garnered significant
interest due to their vast potential, affordability, lightweight portability, ease of
maintenance, and superior maneuverability. These UAVs have been successfully
deployed in various applications such as target search, food delivery, and disaster
rescue [1]. In scenarios such as navigating tight spaces or rapidly locating targets
in earthquake-affected areas, there is a crucial need for quadrotors to exhibit
responsive attitude control and high-speed flight. Accurate trajectory tracking
becomes vital in complex environments; however, achieving this is challenging
due to the strong coupling characteristics inherent in UAV attitude control [2].
Traditional flight controllers using Euler angles often face limitations due to
potential singularity issues [3], prompting the development of quaternion-based
controllers [4]. Nonetheless, these can experience unwinding events during actual
flights, leading to discontinuous trajectories.
Numerous researchers have explored various methods to reduce the nonlinear
effects on quadrotor UAV attitude control. Techniques employed include fuzzy
controllers [5], neural network controllers [6], and sliding mode controllers [7],

among other algorithms. While these methods have mitigated the nonlinear im-
pact on the UAV model, they have also led to a significant increase in compu-
tational burden [8], limiting their practical application.
Compared to other methods, Nonlinear Dynamic Inversion (NDI) is a practi-
cal nonlinear control algorithm. By accurately characterizing the system, NDI
can generate linearized control laws and eliminate all nonlinearities [9,10]. This
method streamlines the control process, enhancing the stability and dependabil-
ity of attitude control. However, NDI is highly sensitive to model mismatches
[11]. Therefore, when utilizing NDI, attitude control performance and robustness
can be improved by increasing modeling accuracy and calibrating the model ap-
propriately.
To effectively handle the nonlinear dynamics and model uncertainty of the
quadrotor UAV, this paper presents an improved geometric control law based on
INDI [12]. In contrast to the approach in [13], which designs attitude controllers
in SO(3), the proposed controller achieves a decoupling of roll/pitch control and
yaw control for the UAV. The roll/pitch controllers are designed based on the
desired direction of total thrust, while the yaw controller is designed based on
the desired direction of yaw motion. This decoupling architecture enables inde-
pendent control of roll/pitch and yaw, enhancing the UAV’s performance during
agile flight.
The following are this paper’s primary contributions: (a) The proposed INDI-
based decoupled geometric controller is simpler to implement for agile flights
than geometric controllers designed within SO(3). (b) The quadrotor UAV’s
high-speed maneuvers induce unknown external disturbances, which are com-
pensated for by the proposed INDI decoupled controller for yaw angle. The
stability of this controller has been proven through Lyapunov stability analysis.
(c) Simulation results verify that, compared to geometric controllers constructed
in SO(3), the proposed controller offers superior performance in terms of agile
flight and system robustness.

5 Conclusion
This paper introduces a decoupled geometric control algorithm based on INDI.
The method allows the quadrotor UAV to achieve agile flight even when facing
unknown external disturbances. In contrast to current non-decoupled geomet-
ric control algorithms, the proposed algorithm effectively linearizes the UAV’s
nonlinear model by employing INDI. This results in enhanced response time and
improved accuracy in tracking the trajectory. Future work will focus on applying
the proposed control algorithm to practical quadrotor UAVs.
